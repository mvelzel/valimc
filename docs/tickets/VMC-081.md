# VMC-081 — Native simulation core and cheap exploration

Status: In progress
Milestone: M3, at the user's request
Depends on: [VMC-080](VMC-080.md); replaces the open sequence of [080h](VMC-080h.md).

The server must hold 20 TPS, and the goal is a small MSPT on the two-vCPU
Hetzner host, not a tick that barely fits. Budgets, measured by the 081a
harness on two schedulers:

- Loaded combat (capture 8's workload: 150 active mobs, about 1,000 loaded
  entities, 441 active chunks): complete owner tick p95 at most 5 ms.
- Exploration at view 32 / simulation 10 while generating: tick p99 at most
  10 ms, and no non-tick owner callback over 5 ms.

## Evidence: captures 10 and 11 (after 081b)

The user's captures of `13373b2d` on the same host, world and 32/10 settings.

  241 active mobs and 1,316 loaded entities (capture 8: about 150 mobs at
  10.6 TPS). Post-adopt collections fell to 4 per minute. The sampled entity
  phase took 83 ms of each tick, about 344 µs per mob against 380 µs before;
  spawning 4.7 ms and blocks 3.9 ms. The stacks stay flat: mob steps 27%
  (AI 11%), tracking 10%, items and ageing 3%. The caps are vanilla's (289
  spawnable chunks); the population is persistent generated animals plus the
  capped categories. Only the native mob core addresses this.
- **Exploration (11).** For its first 30 seconds the player's group still
  held the combat area and ran at 3–10 TPS; after it unloaded, 20 TPS with a
  15–30 ms tick. Generation delivered about 1,000 chunks a minute with dirty
  CPU at 50% of two schedulers: the pool is dirty schedulers minus two, so on

## Diagnosis

1. **The profile is flat.** The top leaf is `:maps.fold_1/4` at 7%; hundreds
   of functions share the rest, mostly `Enum`, `Map`, `Access` and `MapSet`
   over nested immutable maps. No single fix reaches the budget: the cost is
   the representation and how often it is walked.
2. **About 380 µs per active mob per tick.** The entity phase costs 57 ms for
   150 mobs. JVM vanilla and native servers spend roughly 5–30 µs per mob.
   Each mob changes representation several times a tick (entry, body, sensed
   record, AI `Self`, kernel input, native row, result, store), and its turn
   makes several NIF calls with map conversions around each.
3. **Native kernels sit in the middle of the loop.** Physics, block rules,
   light, paths and spawn placement are already Rust. The boundary crosses
   inside every mob turn and every section phase, so the conversion cost is
   paid per mob instead of once per tick.
4. **Render-only state lives in the simulation owner.** View distance 32 puts
   hundreds of sections in the group. Each tick walks every section through
   every phase, whether or not it simulates; the spawning input
   and block-entity contexts are rebuilt by folding maps, and the heap grows
   to half a gigabyte. Its full collections cost 129 ms each, 73 times a minute.
5. **Exploration work blocks the owner.** Light halos (1.3 ms × 2,645 calls),
   generation adoption (18 ms × 172) and collections take about 16 s of owner
   time per minute outside ticks. Vanilla lights chunks before they are full and only
   attaches finished chunks on the main thread.

Compact Elixir records, the group entity frame and further reuse slices (the
080 direction) would plausibly give two to four times. The budget needs 15 to
30 times in the entity phase. That gap is the language and representation
choice for hot simulation state, not a missing cache.

## Decision

Elixir/OTP is the control plane; Rust is the simulation data plane.

- **Rust owns hot simulation state** in resources held by their group
  process: entities and their AI, spatial and tick indexes, entity trackers,
  scheduled and random block ticks, block-entity tickers and spawning, beside
  the block stores, light and physics that are already native. One tick of a
  group is one dirty-scheduler call over its sections' resources (one frame,
  fixing 080h's section-local sensing and pushing). It returns compact results:
  encoded entity packets per chunk, player-bound events, cross-group effects
  and binary log records for what changed.
- **Elixir keeps** processes, supervision, sockets, protocol and login,
  sessions and player input, commands, tickets and admission, the regionizer,
  transfers and fences, the write-ahead log and generation scheduling. Short
  queries and player interactions call the resource on a normal scheduler
  under 1 ms.
- Entity resources are per section, like block stores, so the existing
  `region_give` transfer, merge and split machinery moves them unchanged.
- Vanilla's mob AI is mutable object code. Rust structs with `&mut self`
  follow its goal and brain classes one to one, which makes parity easier
  than threading immutable `Self` and world maps through every goal.

This follows the architecture rules: state lives in a `ResourceArc` owned by
one Elixir process, long work runs on a dirty scheduler, and every native
path keeps Elixir-side parity tests until its Elixir version is deleted.

## Slices

- **081a — Fixed-workload tick harness.** One loopback client in a generated
  vanilla world at the hosted settings, in a combat and an exploration scenario,
  under `MIX_ENV=bench` with `+S 2`. It reports owner tick and callback
  p50/p95/p99, phases, heap and scheduler use, with GC, heap and stack
  diagnostics. It is the gate for every slice; user captures confirm it. Foundation.
- **081b — Elixir quick wins.** Done. Garbage collection was the largest single
  cost: pending generation ticks of rendered chunks now wait frozen off the heap,
  and groups hosting players get a large young heap. Rule reach, spawning inputs,
  tracker projections and chunk encoding stopped walking the whole group. On the
  development Mac: combat tick mean −29%, exploration −41%, spikes gone; the
  hosted combat tick stays near its budget, so the native core is still needed.
- **081c — Constant-cost chunk adoption.** Light completes in the generation
  pipeline with its neighbour dependency, as vanilla's light status does.
  Later edge relighting runs as a worker job on snapshots. Workers encode chunk
  packets. Adopting a generated column attaches native handles and publishes,
  without halo messages through the owner. Render-only sections keep no
  per-chunk Elixir maps. Generation runs on a native pool at lower OS priority
  than the BEAM schedulers, with more than one delivery in flight per group: it
  now runs at the owner's install rate, about one core. Then remove the
  explicit collection after adopts.
- **081d — Native entity sections.** Dense entity storage with generation
  ids, chunk spatial hash, per-chunk tick lists, vanilla's server-entity
  tracker emitting encoded packets, and dirty classes emitting log records.
  Items, orbs, projectiles, falling blocks and primed TNT move first.
- **081e — Native mob core with an ordered fallback.** Living and mob base
  ticks, controls, navigation glue, goal selectors, brains and sensing in Rust.
  The tick runs mobs in id order. For a family not yet ported, it yields that
  mob's compact view to Elixir and resumes after its think, so order is kept
  while families migrate.
- **081f — Port mob families by population.** Passive animals; zombies,
  skeletons, creepers and spiders; villagers and golems; aquatic; Nether; End
  and the dragon. A seeded replay compares the Elixir and native turn for a
  fixed number of ticks before each family's Elixir code is deleted.
- **081g — Native block ticks and spawning.** Scheduled and random ticks,
  block-entity tickers and the whole spawn plan run inside the frame with
  incrementally maintained inputs.
- **081h — Durability and replication from deltas.** The owner forwards the
  core's log records and drops `RegionStorage`'s old/new diffing. Connections
  filter prepared per-chunk frames instead of rebuilding replication maps.

Order: 081a first, then 081b and 081c alongside 081d; 081e through 081h follow.
Multiple players across cores (ownership islands) waits until one group fits
its budget.

Acceptance:

- The harness budgets above hold on two schedulers, and a user capture of
  each workload confirms 20 completed TPS with the budgets' headroom.
- Preserve entity order, RNG consumption, ownership generations, handoffs,
  visibility, mob caps, simulation tickets and atomic durable frames.
- Each slice passes its affected vanilla stages; each ported family passes
  its seeded replay before its Elixir path is removed.
- Native panics fail the group, which is admitted again from its log; no
  `unsafe` outside the existing NIF boundary.
