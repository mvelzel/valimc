## User captures 8 and 9 after eligibility and recovery

The user labels capture 8 as loaded-area movement/combat and capture 9 as
exploration too. Both are completed one-minute captures, starting October 25 at
15:35:52 and 15:37:27 UTC. Filter history to each capture's timestamps; its
900-row array also contains earlier health history. Full-capture owner deltas
are available for the same surviving player owner. Inputs, hashes and extracted
summaries are retained under ignored `.local/simulation-step/profile8-9/`.

| Measurement | Loaded combat (8) | Exploration (9) |
|---|---:|---:|
| Completed owner ticks / capture | 635 / 60.002 s | 315 / 60.033 s |
| Completed simulation TPS | 10.58 | 5.25 |
| Mean complete tick callback | 74.91 ms | 96.49 ms |
| Capture wall time / completed tick | 94.49 ms | 190.58 ms |
| Entity phase / sampled tick | 57.39 ms | 75.68 ms |
| Entity share of sampled tick wall time | 80.2% | 71.5% |
| Explicit collection callbacks | 7 / 0.309 s | 73 / 9.390 s |
| Owner memory, last process sample | 133.5 MiB | 518.5 MiB |
| Final sampled loaded / active chunks | 4,696 / 441 | 11,778 / 441 |
| Final sampled loaded entities / active mobs | 1,016 / 150 | 2,240 / 76 |

Entity phase totals cover 32 and 16 sampled ticks respectively, not every tick.
They include counter work, special entities, AI, movement and replication;
this does not assign 80% to AI alone or separate native time from scheduler
waits/automatic GC. Stack observations corroborate the ordinary mob/AI path but
overlap and truncate; a `Physics.Kernel.error/1` frame does not prove a failure.
Lifetime maxima and the last pooled MSPT ring are not whole-capture percentiles.

The owner spends 99.3% / 97.7% of capture wall time inside measured callbacks.
Combat uses 47.565 s in tick callbacks and about 12.0 s in other callbacks.
Exploration uses 30.394 s in ticks and about 28.2 s elsewhere. The latter includes
9.390 s of explicit full collection, 3.330 s of light and 3.086 s of generation
adoption. Generic calls/resource callbacks and commits consume further time.
The resulting 190.6 ms per completed turn explains worse gameplay than the
96.5 ms mean tick body suggests. The environment clock remains near 20 TPS;
completed simulation throughput is correctly much lower.

`Region.owner_info({:adopt, ...})` calls `collect_soon`; `owner_info(:collect)`
synchronously invokes `:erlang.garbage_collect/0`. In capture 9 these callbacks
average 128.6 ms. Existing coalescing covers only adopts before the queued collect
message runs; exploration still produces repeated whole-owner collections.
That collector was introduced to avoid repeatedly copying adopted state during
minor GC. Simply removing it could trade pauses for more copying or retention.

During exploration sampled owner sections grow from 71 to 232, loaded entities
from 854 to 2,240 at the final sample, while active chunks remain 441. Normal
schedulers average about 50% across two schedulers, consistent with a saturated
serial owner; dirty native work additionally shares the physical CPUs. More
workers cannot make an over-budget ordered entity turn independent. Retained
sections have ticket, in-flight-work and idle-delay rules; growth alone does not
prove a leak or identify which rule holds each section.

Compared with earlier combat/exploration captures, 7.41 / 4.76 TPS becomes about
10.58 / 5.25. These are different runs with differing entity populations and
terrain, and the MSPT scope changed. They show continued inadequate throughput,
not a controlled percentage speedup attributable to one slice.

Next priorities remain a shared active-entity frame with explicit cross-section
turn/effect order and reduced representation churn, plus bounded/coalesced
section adoption and reduced whole-owner heap pressure during exploration.
Measure unload-blocking reasons before changing retention. Elixir should retain
ownership, admission and fences; compare compact Elixir hot state against a
resource-owned native entity/query slice on the same representative workload,
including conversion, publication, memory and latency. Existing native physics
is reused. Removing recorded explicit GC cost alone cannot reach 20 TPS; the
combat entity phase already exceeds the entire 50 ms budget.

The supplied live combat/exploration captures also confirm actual reconnect and
play after 080k's log-recovery fix. No new runtime deployment or client-driven
load test is part of this analysis.
