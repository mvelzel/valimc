# VMC-022 — Heightmaps and light propagation

Status: In progress
Milestone: M2 real worlds
Depends on: [VMC-021](VMC-021.md) (imported worlds, stored light), VMC-065 (published
snapshots for reads across owners).

ValiMC computes no light. Generated chunks are sent full-bright; imported chunks
send the light vanilla stored, which goes stale when blocks change (a torch
placed in a cave lights it only for players who saw it placed).

Scope: compute vanilla's sky and block light for every chunk and keep it current
when blocks change, across chunk and region borders, and send it to clients.

Subtickets:

| Ticket | Slice | Status |
|---|---|---|
| VMC-022a | Generated light facts: emission, dampening, face occlusion per state | Foundation |
| VMC-022b | Rust light engine: batch sky and block light, golden parity with vanilla | Foundation |
| VMC-022c | Incremental relighting on edits, region borders, light packets; client run | In progress |

Acceptance (parent):

- Light facts come from the pinned jar: `getLightEmission`, `getLightDampening`,
  and `LightEngine.getOcclusionShape` per face, with `Shapes.faceShapeOccludes`
  between touching faces, verified exhaustively per equivalence class.
- Rules, from `javap` of the pinned `LightEngine`, `BlockLightEngine` and
  `SkyLightEngine`:
  - Light entering a block loses `max(1, dampening)`.
  - It does not cross a face where the two touching face shapes occlude.
  - Block light starts from emission. Sky light is 15 at and above each
    column's lowest sky source (`ChunkSkyLightSources`, the existing
    `sky_blocks` facts).
  - The result is the unique fixpoint, however it is computed.
- Golden parity: for every chunk of a vanilla world whose eight neighbours are
  full, ValiMC's computed sky and block light equal what vanilla's own light
  engine computes from the same blocks.
- Edits relight incrementally, as vanilla's increase and decrease queues do, also
  across region borders. Clients receive `light_update` for changed sections, and
  chunk packets carry computed light.
- Benchmarked: full relight per chunk and incremental updates per edit.

## VMC-022a/b — facts and batch engine (October 20, 2026)

- `LightFactsOracle` writes `priv/physics/light_blocks.json` (stage
  `light_facts`). For each of 35,723 states it records emission and dampening and
  a face-occlusion class per direction (26–28 classes per direction). Per
  direction there is an occlusion table between a face and the opposite face,
  from `Shapes.faceShapeOccludes` over `LightEngine.getOcclusionShape`.
  11,359,914 checks confirm every state against every class representative.
- `valimc_core::light`: the dampening loss, face occlusion and column sky
  sources follow the existing `sky_blocks` facts. The fixpoint is computed with
  one bucket queue per level, highest first. Sky sources are enqueued only where
  a neighbouring cell is darker: the lowest source, and sources beside lower
  columns. `Valimc.World.Light` installs the facts at start. `light_columns`
  (dirty CPU) lights a rectangle of column images and returns vanilla nibble
  arrays.
- Stage `light`:
  - `ChunkOracle` generates the world, and `LightOracle` feeds its 400 full
    chunks, as ProtoChunks, to vanilla's own `LevelLightEngine` (status updates,
    `setLightEnabled`, `propagateLightSources`, updates run to completion).
  - ValiMC equals vanilla in every one of 63,700,992 sky and block values of the
    324 chunks with full neighbours.
- The light vanilla stores in region files is not a reliable reference, which
  is why the oracle runs the engine:
  - A uniform zero layer is omitted on save (`DataLayer.isEmpty`), just like a
    section with no layer, so absent arrays are ambiguous.
  - Stored light can also be stale: one oak tree's leaves were stored at 15
    beneath them.
- Speed: 400 chunks light in a median 292 ms on one core (about 0.73 ms per
  chunk, including image decoding and nibble packing).
- Tests: `cargo test` (falloff and stops at opaque blocks, sky under overhangs,
  vanilla nibble layout), stages `light_facts` and `light`.
