# VMC-077 — Pre-M3 architecture audit and foundations plan

Status: In progress
Milestone: foundations II (between M2 and M3)
Depends on: [VMC-076](VMC-076.md) (fast checks).

M1 and M2 were built by many sessions in parallel. Before M3 adds mobs, combat,
projectiles, redstone and block updates, this audit asks whether the server is
one coherent model, what makes it unstable or slow at the root, whether Elixir
and Rust each do what they are best at, and what is simply wrong. It compares
each major design choice with vanilla 26.3, Paper, Folia and Pumpkin, and judges
each place where ValiMC differs from vanilla by what the difference buys:
speed, scale, lower resource use, parallelism or robustness. A difference that
buys nothing and causes problems goes; one that pays for itself stays.

Method: eight read-only audits at `7f563f3` (world core and storage, cross-owner
transfers and items, players and protocol, physics, commands, worldgen and the
Rust crates, the Elixir/Rust boundary, system architecture) and three source
studies of the pinned upstreams in `research/` (vanilla 26.3 decompiled, Paper
`a2d844f` and Folia `acf6733`, Pumpkin `b0c48b2d` with Minestom and Valence). The
key bugs and root causes below were confirmed by reading the code. Raw reports
stay in the ignored `.local/code-audit/`.

Sizes at the audit: 67,900 lines of Elixir in 421 modules, 50,100 lines of
Rust, 29,500 lines of tests after VMC-076, 25,500 lines of Java oracles.

## Verdict

At the file level the code is disciplined: ownership, fencing, bounded queues
and parity testing are applied everywhere. At the model level it is not one
design. The same concern is solved again per feature: about 15 cross-owner
protocols, 6 replication styles, 4 job trackers, 4 owner lookup patterns, 3
entity stores, 2 block-rule engines, 2 text codecs and about 15 per-state fact
tables. Four early decisions produce most of this, and M3 would multiply each:

1. **The player is owned by its connection process; everything else by its
   region.** Every pickup, drop, mining drop, placement, chest or furnace click,
   XP pickup and death drop is therefore a distributed transaction (outbox,
   permit, receipt, 2–3 sequential syncs), about 9,000 lines across 55 modules,
   with one transfer in flight per player. M3 combat and mobs would add more.
2. **Every acknowledged action is durable before the reply**, and frames carry
   whole values (all items, all containers) rather than changes. That forced
   the 256-item and 4,096-receipt caps, eight container format versions, held
   replies, and the per-owner logs behind the EMFILE crash loops (VMC-074).
3. **Regions are fixed 8 × 8-chunk tick loops with no border protocol and no
   lifecycle.** Block updates are dropped at every 128-block line, items and
   light each grew their own border mechanism, and regions never unload.
4. **Work is pushed into background jobs with deadlines and region-wide fences**
   instead of running on the owner's tick: movement checks, item motion,
   cooking plans, per-operation planning workers that copy whole region state.
   Any lateness or unrelated block change becomes a teleport back or a dropped
   tick (VMC-014c4, VMC-015e, VMC-017b5 recorded hundreds of corrections).

Rust holds the right M1/M2 work (sections, chunk encoding, worldgen, light, NBT
reading, Anvil) and nothing in Rust owns threads, I/O or orchestration. It is
missing where M3 needs it most: collision and entity simulation, and block-rule
propagation. Its weak point is scheduling: owners call dirty NIFs that queue
behind long worldgen jobs.

Doing the plan below removes roughly 17,000–23,000 lines of Elixir (a quarter to
a third), about 12 fact files and their generators, and whole classes of bugs,
while giving M3 its home: one entity model, one physics kernel, one block-rule
engine and owners that hold everything that interacts.

## How others solve the same problems

| Concern | Vanilla 26.3 | Paper | Folia | Pumpkin | ValiMC today |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Tick threads | One server thread runs every dimension | One main thread; chunk load, generation, light and I/O on pools | Regions tick on a pool (earliest deadline first); a global region for time and weather | Rayon per world; players and entities tick in parallel over shared locks | A process per 8 × 8 region, per connection and for the clock |
| Who owns a player | An entity in its level | Same | An entity of the region it stands in | Shared `Arc` with atomics and mutexes | Its connection process |
| Pickup, chest click, breaking | One method call | Same | One call inside the region | Calls under fine locks, with visible race windows | Durable cross-owner transaction |
| Partition borders | None | None | Regions merge when their chunks come within 3 sections; updates beyond the owned area are dropped | None | Every 128 blocks; neighbour updates dropped |
| Durability | Autosave every 5 min; changed chunks about 10 s later; no commit across files; crash dupes and losses | Chunk fsync off by default; same dupes | Per-region autosave; same | No fsync at all; player files truncated in place | Group-commit fsync before every reply; exactly once |
| Worldgen | Noise and biomes parallel; decoration on one serial queue per dimension; proto chunks saved on unload | Scheduler with a write radius per step, conflicts queue, priority inheritance | Same as Paper | One scheduler thread per dimension moves chunk data to workers; protos saved on unload | Per-region proto owners in a distributed two-phase commit with durable locks, busy and back-off |
| Light | Per chunk at generation, then incremental | Starlight: per chunk with edge checks, parallel queues | Same | Per chunk at generation; runtime light never sent | Whole region relit per generated batch |
| Movement checks | Synchronous; accept unless the move causes a new collision | Same, configurable | Same, per region | None (trusts the client) | Background task; any late or stale result teleports back |
| Entity tracking | Push per entity per interval; encoded per connection | Nearby-player maps; encoded per connection | Same, per region | Encoded once and shared; chunks per viewer | Every observer pulls every peer each 50 ms; encoded once |
| Redstone and updates | Depth-first on one thread | Optional Alternate Current (not vanilla order) | Vanilla order inside a region; never crosses one | Parallel batches, nondeterministic order | Elixir rules live, Rust rules in worldgen; dropped at borders |
| Failure | Crash report, save everything, stop | Bad entities discarded; watchdog | Any region failure stops the server | Any panic stops the server | The owner restarts from its log |
| Chunk lifecycle | Tickets with types and timeouts; unload and save | Staged per-player loader, rate limits | Per region | Ticket levels without types | Regions never unload |

What this shows:

- **Consistency comes from co-location.** Vanilla, Paper and Folia never run a
  protocol for a pickup or a click: the player and the world it touches have one
  owner. Folia, the parallel design closest to ours, keeps that property by
  making the unit of ownership a connected cluster of loaded chunks, so
  ownership boundaries only exist where nothing can interact. It uses messages
  only for rare long-range effects (teleports, pearls, commands aimed at other
  players). ValiMC pays a durable transaction for exactly the frequent ones.
- **Nobody else makes gameplay durable per action.** Everyone else accepts
  crash dupes. Durability beyond vanilla is a real robustness gain worth keeping,
  but it does not need a sync per action: one atomic frame per owner per tick
  gives "no dupes, at most a tick or two lost" at a fraction of the cost.
- **Generation without distributed locks.** Vanilla serializes decoration per
  dimension; Moonrise and Pumpkin use one scheduler per dimension that hands
  out non-overlapping write areas and queues conflicts. All three treat proto
  chunks as recomputable (generation is deterministic). Pumpkin's 1.35× live
  generation lead is this orchestration, not faster Rust: on one thread the two
  servers are level, and ValiMC spends about 1.3× the CPU per area encoding
  proto chunks between owners and logging them durably.
- **Where ValiMC is already ahead**, and should stay ahead: broadcasts and chunk
  payloads are encoded once (vanilla and Paper encode per connection); ingress
  and mailboxes are bounded (vanilla's queues are unbounded); the region log is
  far stronger than any upstream's saving; a crashed owner restarts from its log
  while the rest of the server keeps running (Folia, vanilla and Pumpkin stop the
  whole server); generation kernels match Pumpkin per thread; tick order inside
  an owner is deterministic and steppable in tests (Pumpkin's is not).

## Decisions

Four choices change the plan's shape. The user reviewed the audit on October
21, 2026 and decided: D1 option C, D2 per-tick frames, D3 volatile proto
chunks, and for D4 no migration (existing worlds are deleted). The plan below
follows these decisions; the rest of the audit's recommendations were accepted
as written.

**D1. The unit of ownership for gameplay.**

| Option | What it is | Buys | Costs |
|---|---|---|---|
| A (today) | Player in its connection; fixed 8 × 8 regions | Each player's tick on its own core | Every interaction is a transaction; borders every 128 blocks break block updates, redstone and combat; M3 multiplies both |
| B | Players owned by the region they stand in; fixed 8 × 8 regions with a lockstep tick and a border protocol | In-region interactions become calls | Within reach of a border (up to about 18 % of positions) interactions stay cross-owner; redstone and updates crossing a line are a tick late or need synchronous hops |
| **C (recommended)** | Players owned by their region, and regions become dynamic groups of 8 × 8 sections that merge when their loaded chunks come near (Folia's rule) and split when apart | Everything that can interact has one owner, as in vanilla: pickups, clicks, combat, redstone, block updates and mob AI are plain calls in vanilla order; the transaction engine shrinks to logins, handoffs and teleports | A crowd in one place runs on one core (as in Folia, and as vanilla runs everything); a regionizer per dimension; merge and split hand state between processes |

C keeps ValiMC's parallel gain where it is real: separate groups of players
tick on separate cores, as on Folia, and per-player work that touches no shared
state (decoding, encryption, compression, chunk streaming, the pure part of
movement checks against published snapshots) stays in connection processes. The
8 × 8 section stays the unit of storage and generation. Sections move between
group processes by passing their Rust resource and ETS tables, which copies
nothing large.

**D2. The durability contract.** Recommended: each owner commits its tick's
changes as one atomic frame (one group commit per tick into a shared, striped
log) and replies without waiting for the sync. A crash loses at most the last
tick or two, never splits an interaction (both halves are in one owner's frame)
and never duplicates. Still far stronger than every upstream; removes held
replies, per-action syncs and the latency they add. Alternative: keep replies
waiting for durability (today's guarantee) on the new log.

**D3. Proto chunk durability.** Recommended: proto chunks are volatile (kept in
memory, snapshotted at shutdown, regenerated deterministically after a crash),
as in vanilla, Paper and Pumpkin; only full chunks are durable. After a hard
crash a feature crossing into a not-yet-full neighbour could regenerate
differently at the edge of the generated area, as in vanilla. Alternative: keep
proto chunks durable, at the cost of the log traffic measured above.

**D4. Old worlds.** Decided: the storage change in phase 2 breaks the region
log format once with no migration; worlds written before it are deleted, an old
log is refused with a clear error, and no legacy readers stay in `lib/`.

## Findings by root cause

Each finding names its evidence briefly; file references are at 7f563f3.

**Cross-owner effects (decision 1).**
- `Txn.Operation` covers 5 player-sourced kinds; items, handoffs, merges,
  cooking orbs, XP, proto finalize and portals each run their own protocol, with
  per-kind permits (5 modules), request codecs (5 hand-rolled binary envelopes),
  receipt tables (3 digest schemes kept for disk compatibility) and 4 retry
  loops. `Region` exposes about 45 functions, 23 of RegionData's 50
  `handle_call` clauses are reserve, apply, release or receipt messages.
- XP orbs are not entities but a cooking ledger inside the containers record
  (2,130 lines; containers format v1–v8); XP pickup is encoded as a container
  request. Items and orbs share only their view and publication; mobs would
  become a third model.
- Permits are wall-clock grants (pickup 250 ms) that span a player-log sync;
  `owner_call_scale` exists because deadlines miss under load (VMC-073).

**Persistence (decision 2).**
- Frames carry absolute values: every item change writes all items and
  receipts (up to 750 KB), every click or orb pickup all containers (up to 8 MiB;
  172–228 KB measured per orb pickup); a region with an active furnace encodes
  all containers twice per tick.
- Block persistence is a sparse cell map over column images that is never
  folded (`Store` keeps every edited cell forever and rewrites it in every
  checkpoint); loading replays the history.
- Four durable shapes: per-region logs, per-proto logs, the shared player log,
  and ad hoc JSON files (`world.json` rewritten in place, `ClockStore`,
  `ForcedChunks`, `Stopwatches`). Per-owner logs need `FileBudget`.

**Regions (decision 1).**
- Neighbour and shape updates stop at the region (`in_region?`;
  `Block.Support` treats another region's neighbour as never breaking anything).
- Regions never unload (backlog VMC-013): memory and tick CPU grow with explored
  area until `max_regions` stops admission for everyone.
- A region whose load always fails is re-admitted on every access with no
  backoff. The top supervisor is one flat `rest_for_one` of 35 children, so an
  early infrastructure crash restarts every region and connection.

**Background jobs, deadlines and fences.**
- Each move packet spawns a collision task (pool sized 2 × max players, so it
  bounds nothing), then corrects on any deadline, signature or fence mismatch;
  vanilla validates synchronously and accepts a "moved wrongly" position unless
  it causes a new collision. Physics readers fence on the region-wide revision,
  so any block change anywhere in the region discards results.
- `RegionData` (4,643 lines, 58 fields, 127 message clauses) runs six bespoke
  worker launchers that copy whole items, containers or receipts per mutation;
  one planning slot per region turns contention into busy errors, retries and
  finally disconnects.
- Commands, chunk requests (up to 15 s) and creative placement call owners
  synchronously from the connection; `guard_connection` catches raises but not
  exits, so a slow owner kills the session.

**Lighting and generation.**
- Installing generated columns over the flat base profile changes more than
  `RELIGHT_LIMIT` (16,384) cells per chunk, so every batch recomputes light for
  the whole region plus its ring (a 19.6 MB copy, about 73 ms on a dirty
  scheduler per four-chunk batch where per-chunk light needs about 3 ms). A
  likely large part of the 10–35 s chunk latency after long teleports.
- Worldgen and decoration pools are each sized to the dirty scheduler count,
  so generation alone subscribes them twice, and owners' dirty calls (light,
  loot) queue behind jobs of 100 ms or more. `/locate` and `/place` run dirty
  NIFs inside the connection, outside any pool.
- Structure starts and their flags live in per-state Rust caches rather than as
  chunk state the scheduler owns.

**Duplicated models and facts.**
- About 15 generated per-state physics tables, each with its own generator and
  encoding, plus separate Rust flag tables; `canSurvive` exists twice (1.5 MB
  and 1.1 MB JSON); the tick callbacks are declared twice.
- Five block-read mechanisms, six replication styles (pull for peers and
  entities, push for blocks and events, diffs for the environment), three
  "enter a level" copies, two text-component codecs, two item-component
  decoders, 34 private `f32` helpers, 75 modules decoding `priv` JSON.
- The connection is one 92-key map edited by about 20 modules; 14 flags and
  three resume chains serialize a player's actions; the player has no schema
  (its durable fields are listed in four places that already diverged).
- Living rules (health, damage, effects, air, burning) are about 20 player-only
  modules with hard-coded damage sources; mobs need all of them.
- `commands.ex` maps 396 full vanilla paths to handlers through about 60
  trampolines; the command engine depends on the catalog and the catalog on the
  socket.
- The largest compile cycle spans 177 files; about 69 public functions are
  called only by tests (five of them `Connection` calls).

**Elixir and Rust.**
- Right in Rust today: sections and chunk encoding, worldgen (per-thread level
  with Pumpkin), light, disk NBT, Anvil. Right in Elixir: sockets and codecs
  (`:zlib` and `:crypto` are native already), auth, transactions, persistence
  orchestration, inventories, chat and commands.
- Too little Rust: player collision costs 0.26–0.94 ms per move in Elixir and
  saturates ten cores near 650 players; item motion 23–37 µs per item step;
  block behaviours read neighbours with one NIF call per position.
- NIF hygiene: 36 of 72 NIFs are dirty with ad hoc dispatch; errors mix about
  43 atom and 42 string conventions; 16 parity or bench NIFs ship in production;
  `lib.rs` is one 2,392-line file; snapshots copy whole chunks into BEAM terms
  (one byte per cell, about twice the Rust memory) on every batch.

## Plan

Phases replace the four root decisions in order; each slice is a coherent
ticket of 300–1,500 lines with its own acceptance. Checks per slice follow
AGENTS.md; parity comes from the existing oracle stages.

**Phase 0 — stop the damage (small, now).** Two high bugs fixed by deleting
or narrowing code: a held merge-counters reply applies its counters to the
items as they are when it is released instead of restoring the plan's
snapshot, and a position without an observed block entity reads as vanilla's
absent one (shulker boxes collide as full blocks, moving pistons as empty;
the `:unavailable` kind and its errors are gone). The full-region kick and the
cooking award cap stay for VMC-077h: their fix is the keyed entity store,
and a patch now would add mechanism to code that slice deletes. Everything
else waits for the slice that replaces its code.

**Phase 1 — clear wins that need no decision.**
- VMC-077a Runtime safety: tiered supervisors (infrastructure, storage, world
  per dimension, players, network) with table ownership at start, listener and
  region-load backoff, an immutable `World.Spec` loaded once with atomic
  settings writes, explicit dimension keys instead of the process-dictionary
  context, connections that never call owners synchronously, commands as jobs,
  per-phase frame caps.
- VMC-077b Dirty-scheduler policy and NIF hygiene: one worldgen pool capped
  below the dirty scheduler count, microsecond NIFs on normal schedulers,
  bounded or resumable light and halo work, `/locate` and `/place` through the
  pool; `lib.rs` split by domain, parity NIFs behind a cargo feature, one arity
  and one error convention, owner checks on mutable resources.
- VMC-077c Light: per-chunk light at generation in Rust (Starlight's shape:
  column fill for sky, edge checks against lit neighbours), persisted with the
  column image, incremental afterwards, light packets with section masks.
- VMC-077d One block fact table: one generated per-state table (shape, motion,
  fluid, climb, contact, hazard, heightmap, mining, survival) shared by Elixir
  and Rust; delete the per-feature `*Blocks` modules, duplicate JSON and their
  generators; data-driven damage types and enchantment effects.
- VMC-077e Chunk lifecycle: tickets (view, simulation, forced, portal,
  teleport) drive load, tick and unload; only simulation distance ticks;
  vanilla's ack-paced chunk batches; `World.View`, one lazy read view over
  published snapshots that also carries heightmaps and biomes.

**Phase 2 — storage and owner foundations (D2, D4).**
- VMC-077f Log: one striped write-ahead log shared by every owner (the player
  log's shape), typed delta operations keyed by record, per-section checkpoint
  files built from column images, one commit per owner per tick, one
  acknowledgement; delete `FileBudget`, whole-value frames, format versions and
  the cell map; no migration (D4).
- VMC-077g Owner skeleton: one job table, one admission guard, one error
  taxonomy (retry, reject, conflict, invalid) decided where the error is made;
  `RegionData` split into blocks, block entities, entities and commit state
  behind narrow APIs.

**Phase 3 — one owner for everything that interacts (D1).**
- VMC-077h Entity store: one store per owner for items, orbs and later mobs and
  projectiles, with stable network ids, keyed persistence and push replication
  of per-tick deltas encoded once; orbs leave the containers record.
- VMC-077i Players in owners: the owner holds the player's body, inventory and
  living state; the connection keeps sockets, codecs, chunk streaming and the
  pure pre-checks; pickups, drops, clicks, mining and placement become calls;
  one handoff for logins, crossings and teleports; delete the per-feature
  transfer families; one `Player.State` schema and one menu click engine.
- VMC-077j Regionizer: groups of 8 × 8 sections per dimension merge and split by
  Folia's rule, with epochs, resource handoff and one global tick number; one
  commit covers a group's sections.
- VMC-077k Living: entity-agnostic health, damage, effects, attributes, armor,
  air and burning, shared by players and mobs.

**Phase 4 — simulation kernels in Rust.**
- VMC-077l Physics kernel: collide, step, fluids and support for many bodies
  per call over the owner's sections; movement checks run synchronously with
  vanilla's rules; item and orb motion step in the tick pipeline; Elixir stays
  the parity reference.
- VMC-077m Block-rule engine: shape updates, survival, neighbour updates in
  vanilla order and redstone wire over the owner's sections, shared with
  worldgen; Elixir keeps behaviours with cross-owner effects (containers,
  portals, drops).

**Phase 5 — generation and commands.**
- VMC-077n Generation scheduler: one per dimension, owning chunk statuses and
  proto images as Rust resources that move to workers, write areas that queue
  instead of refusing, priority by distance to players, structure starts as
  scheduler state; proto owners, their logs and the two-phase commit go (D3).
- VMC-077o Commands: one registration per command, a pure command source
  (entity, dimension, position, sink), targets resolved over published
  projections, box edits evaluated atomically inside the owning group.

**Phase 6 — layout.** Domain contexts (data, protocol, net, session, living,
player, entity, blocks, menus, physics, world, storage, worldgen, commands,
admin) with an xref layering check in the audit script; docs describe the new
model.

Order and parallelism: phase 0 and the five phase 1 slices are independent.
Phase 2 precedes phase 3; VMC-077l and VMC-077n touch separate code and can run
beside phases 2–3. M3 starts on the entity store, the physics kernel, the
block-rule engine and the living module.

Expected size, by area: transfers, items and containers 7,000–9,000 lines;
players and connection 2,000–2,500; physics 3,000 plus 600 Java and 12 JSON
files; world core 2,500–3,000; worldgen 1,700; commands 2,000; mechanical
helpers 1,000. Rust grows by the physics kernel and the live use of the
worldgen block rules.

## Elixir and Rust for M3

| M3 area | Where | Boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Mob, item, orb and projectile physics | Rust kernel | One call per owner tick for all bodies, over the owner's sections |
| Pathfinding | Rust | Node budget per tick; reads the group's sections |
| Mob goals and brains | Elixir first, as dense per-owner state (no process per mob) | Moves to Rust only if a matched profile says so |
| Spawning | Candidates in Rust (light, heightmap, biome, shapes are in the resource); caps in Elixir | Per-owner budgets from published counts |
| Combat and damage rules | Elixir (`Living`), events within one owner | No cross-owner hits inside a group |
| Redstone, fluids, block updates | Rust block-rule engine | Emits a change list for the owner's commit |
| Inventories, containers, hoppers | Elixir | Same owner as the blocks |
| Replication | Elixir push of deltas encoded once | Per-chunk nearby-player index |
| Chunk frames | Compressed once per chunk revision, shared | AES stays per connection |

Kept where they are: sockets and codecs, `:zlib` and `:crypto`, auth,
persistence orchestration, generation orchestration, chat and commands.

`minecraft-tester`, the frozen release of `b8646a33` (waves 1 and 2), Java 26.3
by Quick Play, a new vanilla world of seed 20261020 at view distance 12;
evidence in ignored `.local/client-tests/VMC-077-interim/`. Server logs held no

inventory, names and position; unload after a 20,000-block hop and return.

- A solid `/fill` wrote only the box's shell on the server while the client
  showed it full (VMC-077o).
- A ladder could be placed against the free side of another ladder, with no
