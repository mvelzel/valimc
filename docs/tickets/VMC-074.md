# VMC-074 — Bounded file descriptors

Status: Done
Milestone: M2 real worlds
Depends on: [VMC-063](VMC-063.md) (region logs), [VMC-025](VMC-025.md) (proto
owners, VMC-025d), [VMC-070](VMC-070.md) (view distance 10).

Playing a decorated vanilla world on `main` (October 21, 2026), the server ran
out of file descriptors (`EMFILE`, "Too many open files") while the player flew
over new terrain, and fell into crash loops:

- Proto log writers died on `{:full_sync, "Too many open files (os error 24)"}`;
  their owners stopped, restarted, and failed again in their load with a
  `MatchError` on `{:proto_log_recovery_failed, :emfile}`; generation parts
  could not be delivered.
- Region stores failed their claim (`{:mining_recovery_failed, :emfile}` and
  the like) and region owners crashed in their load with a `MatchError`,
  restarted on every request, about 20 times a second.
- The player log, a permanent child of the application's `rest_for_one`
  supervisor, stopped on a failed append, which restarted every child after it:
  region stores, regions, connections and the listener. The player was
  disconnected.
- With no descriptors left the code server could not load the modules the
  logger needed to report the errors.

Cause: macOS starts processes at a soft limit of 256 open files (the JVM raises
it for vanilla, so vanilla never sees this). Every region store, proto log
writer and player log held its log segment open for its lifetime, and opened
more for `F_FULLFSYNC` and directory syncs; proto owners lingered about five
minutes after their work. At view distance 10, with 12 × 12 generation locks,
exploring held well over 256.

Acceptance:

- No log holds a descriptor between operations; what logs open at once is
  bounded by a budget sized from the process's limit, configurable.
- The soft open-files limit is raised at start toward the hard limit, as the
  JVM does, and the effective limit and budget are logged.
- Proto owners stop soon after their region is final and handed over.
- Running out of descriptors is transient: appends, recovery and loads back
  off and retry; nothing crashes with a `MatchError` on a recovery error; a
  log append that wrote nothing never stops its writer, so the listener is
  not restarted.
- Tests run logs under a budget of 4 and with every open failing (`EMFILE`)
  and show the server keeps going and finishes the work once descriptors are
  back.

## Design

- `Valimc.FileBudget`: an atomic counter (no process) of descriptors the logs
  hold. `with_files/2` takes descriptors before a file operation, backing off
  while the budget is full. The cap is half of the raised limit after a
  reserve of 64 for sockets and the runtime, between 8 and 1024
  (`:open_files_budget`, `VALIMC_OPEN_FILES_BUDGET`). `transient?/1` recognises
  `EMFILE`/`ENFILE` in file errors, native errors and nested error tuples;
  `retry/2` backs off from 10 ms to 500 ms for up to five seconds by default.
  `init!/0` raises the soft `RLIMIT_NOFILE` (NIF `set_open_files_limit`: at
  most the hard limit and macOS's `OPEN_MAX` 10240; 10240 on this Mac). It also
  loads the runtime's own modules (kernel, stdlib, elixir, logger: about 500,
  under 0.2 s), so reporting an error never needs a file. A release loads them
  at boot anyway.
- `Valimc.World.RegionLog`: an append opens the segment, writes, `datasync`s
  and closes it; reads, listings, truncation, checkpoints, `F_FULLFSYNC` and
  directory syncs each hold budget descriptors only while they run, retrying
  `EMFILE`. An append whose segment would not open for want of descriptors
  wrote nothing, so it returns `{:error, {:unwritten, reason}}`, and the
  writer may append again. Any other failure after the open may have torn a
  frame and still needs recovery. A directory that cannot be listed no longer
  passes for an empty one: recovery used to treat that as a log with no frames
  and append over the existing frames. File errors come back as
  `{:error, {:file, reason, path}}`, not exceptions.
- Writers: the proto log writer retries an unwritten append, backing off up to
  2 s, so its owner only waits. A region store acknowledges an unwritten
  commit as failed (owners save again) and stays. Any failed append that may
  have torn a frame now stops the store, for calls as for commits. The player
  log replies with the error and stays; after a failure that may have torn a
  frame it reopens the log (recovery) on the next commit instead of
  restarting under the application supervisor.
- Loads: a proto owner's recovery and a region's store open retry transient
  failures for up to 30 s. Otherwise they stop with `{:shutdown, reason}`
  rather than a `MatchError`. Owners are temporary and started on demand, so
  a waiting load is the backoff: callers queue on the loading owner.
- Proto owners: one whose own chunks are all final and handed over retires 5 s
  later, once nothing is in flight; it checks again every 5 s until then.
  Neighbours start it again on demand from its log. Other idle owners keep the
  ten quiet rounds of 30 s.

## Evidence (October 21, 2026)

- `test/open_files_test.exs`: the budget never exceeds its cap with 24
  concurrent users; 300 logs open and append under a budget of 4 with no
  descriptor left open (counted in `/dev/fd`). With the soft limit at 3 (every
  open fails with `EMFILE`) and restored after 300 to 400 ms:
  - an append lands once;
  - a log that cannot be listed keeps its frames;
  - a proto log write is saved and the writer stays;
  - a new region loads, then a block is set and saved, without a crash or a
    code loading error.
  The NIF raises the limit (10240 here), lowers it, and raises it again.
- `test/worldgen_proto_owner_test.exs`: a handed-over owner stops normally
  within seconds.
- Region store, player log, mining, item and container store suites (176
  tests, among them the injected disk failures, `TestClient.break_writer`,
  which now moves the segment out of reach) and the proto and decorated world
  suites (18 tests) pass.
