## Results

1. **A playing-dead axolotl's healing: matches vanilla.** A region trial with
   a real attacker reproduced the extra healing. In a two-deep pool the
   axolotl drifts to the surface while playing dead, as its move control
   holds its last inputs (`AxolotlMoveControl` skips its tick). There it
   leaves the water, `PlayDead.canStillUse` fails (`isInWater`), and the
   behaviour starts again with a fresh Regeneration. To check this against
   vanilla, `mob_ai` gained the oracle case `axolotl_play_dead_shallow`: four
   hits in a two-deep pool, 400 ticks. Vanilla's trace heals about 5 over
   its play-dead window there with no restart, and ours equals it tick for
   tick. The client run's extra healing came from that scene's earlier
   movement, through code that matches vanilla, so nothing changed.
2. **`/effect … saturation`.** Saturation is live. As vanilla's
   instantaneous effects do, its duration is in ticks (1 by default), and it
   feeds a player (`FoodData.eat(amplifier + 1, 1.0)`) each tick it has left.
3. **`/gamerule mob_griefing`.** Rules beyond the three the clock holds are
   kept in the world's `settings.json` (`Valimc.World.Settings`'s
   `game_rules`, published in `:persistent_term` after a durable write).
   `GameRules.get/1` reads them, with a test's override first.
   `mob_griefing` is the first such rule; VMC-006a adds the rest.
4. **`/item replace entity <targets> <slots> with <item> [<count>]`.** The
   `slot_source` argument accepts 26.3's slot names (`slot.unknown`
   otherwise). A player's hotbar, inventory, container, weapon and armour
   slots, and a mob's equipment (`mob_section_set_slot`), take the item.
   Vanilla's feedback is used, including `commands.item.target.failed` when
   no slot took it. `/item replace block`, `… from` and `/item modify` wait
   for block containers and loot modifiers.
