# VMC-077j1 — Group processes own sections; section handoff

Status: Done
Milestone: foundations II (between M2 and M3)
Depends on: [VMC-077](VMC-077.md) (decision D1, option C; findings B8, C3,
C8), [VMC-077e](VMC-077e.md) (tickets and unload), [VMC-077f](VMC-077f.md)
(the shared log), [VMC-077g](VMC-077g.md) (owner skeleton).

Before: the unit of ownership was a fixed 8 × 8 region: one owner process,
one log stream, one tick clock and one published table per region, so every
interaction that crossed a 128-block line crossed processes.

After: an 8 × 8 section stays the unit of storage, generation and light, and
a **group process** owns a set of sections (`Valimc.World.Region`). This
slice builds the group and the handoff that moves sections between groups;
[VMC-077j2](VMC-077j2.md) adds the regionizer that decides which sections
share a group.

Design:

- **Sections in a group.** A group holds each section as the
  `Valimc.World.RegionData` it was (blocks, block entities, entities,
  storage, published table, epoch) and runs every call and message on the
  section it names: by the section's epoch (almost every call carries the
  `{pid, epoch}` handle callers get from the Directory), by a chunk or
  position, or by key (tickets, halo messages and the Directory's own
  queries now carry it). A newly admitted section starts in a group of its
  own.
- **One time.** A group runs one tick over all its sections, in key order,
  numbered from the world clock's game time (`Valimc.World.GameTick`, the
  one game time vanilla keeps for the server), never behind its own last
  tick. Sections loading or handed in continue at the group's tick (or the
  group at a handed-in section's, when that is later: entity frames are
  numbered by it and never go back); their scheduled ticks keep their
  delays, as vanilla's do across an unload. Tests' `:manual` groups count
  their own ticks.
- **One commit.** Sections cut frames into an outbox; the group sends every
  section's frames from one cut as one record of the shared log
  (`Log.commit_group/4`), on its own stripe, so a change that spans sections
  is durable whole or not at all. Acknowledgements name the section's stream.
  A section's frames may now sit on several stripes: a section that joins a
  group on another stripe sends there only once its frames in flight are
  durable, and recovery and pruning read every region stripe.
- **Handoff** (`Region.hand_over/3`, `Region.split/2`), at the source's tick
  boundary (a group handles it between ticks): the section's Rust store
  changes owner (`region_give`: the NIF's owner check now follows a mutable
  owner pid), its table moves with `:ets.give_away/3`, its state is copied in
  one message, the giving group moves the route to the adopter
  (`Directory.move/4`, which checks the adopter owns the table), and the
  adopting group watches the ticket holders and container viewers itself and
  asks the generation scheduler for its chunks as their requester. The route
  goes with the adopter's death from that moment, adopted or not, so a
  section lost in flight loads again from its log. The section keeps its
  epoch: a move is not a new incarnation, so receipts and handles stay valid.
  The group it left forwards what still arrives for it (calls as they came,
  epoch- or key-tagged messages, its workers' reports and ends, log
  acknowledgements, checkpoint reports) for 120 s, never to an adopter that
  stopped; a group left without sections then stops. The adopter runs one
  full collection once a merge's sections are in: until then every minor
  collection copied the received state again, and a moved section ticked at
  twice its cost (about 300 against 150 µs with 64 items).
- **Light** inside a group: halo messages name their target section, and a
  sibling's arrive through the group's own mailbox (light one step late is
  vanilla-equivalent).
- **Failure.** A group that crashes takes its sections down; each is admitted
  again from its log, in a group of its own.
- **Generation.** A section asks the scheduler as `{group pid, key}`, so
  sections of one group keep separate wants.
- **Keys** (finding C8, deferred from VMC-077a). A section's routing key is
  `{dimension, sx, sz}` in every dimension, the overworld included: the
  Directory's routes, `Valimc.RegionRegistry`, the visibility topics and the
  spatial index use it, and the world API (`World.owner/1`, `region/1`,
  `existing_owner/1`, `item_snapshot/1`, `entity_frames/2`, `publish/2`,
  `subscribe/2`; `chunk/2`, `sendable/3`, `chest_lid_snapshot/2` and
  `View.new/1` take the dimension) refuses a plain `{sx, sz}`, so no route
  falls back to the overworld. `Dimension.scope/1` and `unscope/1` are gone.
  Inside a group a section stays `{sx, sz}` (a group is one dimension), and
  durable records keep their plain keys beside the dimension they name
  (player outboxes, entity-handoff addresses, storage owners).

Acceptance:

- A group with two sections routes each call to its section, ticks both on
  one tick number, commits both in one log record and reloads both after a
  crash (`region_group_test.exs`).
- A section handed to another group keeps its epoch, blocks and route; a
  handle held from before is forwarded; only the new group can change its
  Rust store; a split moves sections into a new group; a section whose
  adopter dies before adopting it loads again from its log.
- Light crosses section borders inside one group both ways
  (`region_light_test.exs`).

Checks: groups and handoff, see [VMC-077j3](VMC-077j3.md) (one commit for
the three slices). Keys (their own commit, on fefdca2c): `mix test.fast` 474
of 474, `mix test --only slow --only tcp` 200 of 200, `check_vanilla.py
--changed` 51 of 51 selected stages; `bench/regionizer.exs` runs unchanged.
The type checker found most callers that still passed `{sx, sz}`; the slow
suites found a delivery of a Nether player's outbox that would have gone to
the overworld section (`TransferDelivery.start/5` now takes the dimension).

Client: none per slice; the VMC-077 milestone run covers play across
sections and groups.

Deferred:

- The process's dimension context stays for what is not routing: the
  dimension's facts (`Chunk.valid_y?/1`, `Chunk.base/1`,
  `Dimension.facts/0`), a view's default dimension and the dimension work in
  a bound task routes in (`Dimension.bind/1`); an unbound task still reads
  the overworld's facts. Removing it means passing the dimension to every
  block-position validator.
- Calls and messages that carry neither an epoch, a chunk nor a key reach
  every section of a group (`{:checkpointed, ...}`, worker ends): each acts
  only on its own. A group of many sections pays a little per such message.
