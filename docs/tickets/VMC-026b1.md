# VMC-026b1 — Dimension lifecycle

Status: Foundation
Milestone: M2 real worlds
Depends on: [VMC-026a](VMC-026a.md), [VMC-025](VMC-025.md) (live decoration,
VMC-025d).

The Nether and the End become live worlds beside the overworld, and a player
can change dimension keeping its state. Portals (VMC-026b2–b4) make this
reachable in play; until then a command teleport to a player in another
dimension is the only way across, so the slice is a foundation.

Acceptance:

- Each dimension has its own region and proto owners, stores and generator;
  equal region keys of two dimensions never meet. The Nether and the End store
  under `dimensions/minecraft/the_nether` and `the_end`, as 26.3's
  `DimensionType.getStorageFolder` lays them out (the `DIM-1`/`DIM1` folders
  are older versions' layout).
- Their chunks generate and decorate live as vanilla's do, region by region
  (oracle stages `worldgen_live_nether` and `worldgen_live_end`); a flat
  world's Nether and End generate as vanilla's flat preset keeps them.
- The client gets the three levels at login, each dimension's type, hashed
  seed and sea level, chunks of 16 sections in the Nether and the End, and no
  sky light in the Nether.
- A travel resends the level as vanilla's cross-level teleport does (respawn
  keeping all data, then position, abilities, border, clocks, spawn, rain,
  inventory, effects, experience and health) and keeps the player's
  inventory, health, effects and game mode; players see each other only in the
  same dimension.
- The player's dimension, its last death's dimension and its portal cooldown
  persist; a reconnect rejoins in the dimension it left. A death elsewhere
  respawns in the overworld with the death location in the dimension of death.
- Weather is the overworld's only; each dimension has its own world border,
  forced chunks and default clock (the Nether has none) as vanilla scopes them.

## Design

- `Valimc.Dimension`: the three dimensions with their facts from
  `dimensions.json` (which `WorldgenFactsOracle` now also fills with each
  dimension type's sky light, ceiling, fixed time, dragon fight, coordinate
  scale, ambient light, infiniburn tag and default clock). Each process works
  in one dimension, its context: region, store and proto owners take theirs
  at start, a connection its player's, and `Dimension.bind/1` carries it into
  tasks (chunk encoding, queries, deliveries). Routing (the directory, the
  owner registries, visibility topics, the player index) scopes keys by the
  caller's context; the overworld's scoped keys stay the plain keys, so no
  overworld record or key changed.
- Region keys and durable records are unchanged inside a dimension; a record
  names a region of the store that holds it. A player's pending transfer
  targets the dimension of its snapshot, and a player changes dimension only
  with nothing in flight, so the player store delivers in that dimension.
- Storage: every dimension's chunks keep the overworld's 24 sections; the
  Nether's and the End's y 0 to 255 are sections 4 to 19 and the rest stays
  air (their base profile is air). Packets send the dimension's window:
  `encode_chunk` takes the window and writes heights relative to its bottom,
  light packets carry 18 sections and no sky arrays in the Nether. The region
  light engine has a no-sky switch (`light_sky`), set for the Nether.
- Proto logs v2 hold a chunk's own bounds and the entities features add (the
  End crystals), and end gateway block entities carry their exits into the
  full chunk's NBT. `Generator.worldgen/2` keeps one native state per seed and
  noise settings.
- Build height, void damage, block snapshots and `Chunk.base/1` follow the
  caller's dimension. `World.publish/2` tags Nether and End events with their
  dimension so a connection that just left drops stale ones.
- `Valimc.Player.Travel`: a travel waits until the old dimension's work
  settles (saves, transfers, recovery, placement/pickup/mining/experience
  jobs, menus), saves the player at the destination, then leaves the old
  level (unsubscribes, moves the player in the index, tells the old peers) and
  rebuilds the client's. A respawn after a death in another dimension leaves
  the same way. `/tp` to a player in another dimension, or a location in the
  source's, travels; command travel starts no portal cooldown, portal travel
  will (10 ticks, vanilla's `getDimensionChangingDelay`).
- Snapshot v22 wraps a base version with the dimension, the death's dimension
  and the portal cooldown; overworld players off cooldown keep their version.

## Evidence

- `test/dimension_travel_connection_test.exs`: travel to the Nether (respawn
  with keep 3, the Nether's type and sea level 32, 16-section chunks without
  sky light, the Nether's region owner and folder, the arrival saved first),
  a reconnect rejoins there, travel back; a death in the Nether respawns in
  the overworld with a Nether death location; players at the same coordinates
  in different dimensions do not see each other and do again once together.
- `test/dimension_test.exs`: facts, scoping and storage folders, task binding,
  build height and base profile per dimension, snapshot v22 round trips and
  the overworld's unchanged bytes, light packet windows.
- Rust: windowed chunk encoding and heights; no sky light in a skyless area,
  also after an edit opens the floor.
- Oracle stages (October 21, 2026): `worldgen_live_nether` and
  `worldgen_live_end` (new; `DecorationOracle … nether|end live`) pass: 384
  final chunks each, the same three live sequences as the overworld's, equal
  vanilla's in blocks, marks and ticks (these areas hold no block entities;
  gateway exits are VMC-026b4's).

## Limits

- No cross-dimension `/clone` (the source's dimension only), no `execute in`
  (VMC-053), and `/setworldspawn` keeps the spawn in the overworld (vanilla
  stores its dimension; respawn points elsewhere are VMC-040).
- Items, orbs and other entities do not travel yet; region entities stay in
  their dimension.
- End crystals from decoration are stored in proto logs but not spawned as
  live entities (they need the entity model's crystals, M3).
- The first login after a cold start can take seconds: the
  `Valimc.Commands` module is about 108 MB of BEAM (VMC-053's precomputed
  tree) and loads on the first command tree packet.
