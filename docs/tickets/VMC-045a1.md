# VMC-045a1 — End crystals

Status: Awaiting client
Milestone: M3
Depends on: [VMC-050a1](VMC-050a1.md), [VMC-037o](VMC-037o.md).

Generated spike crystals and crystals placed on obsidian or bedrock are live
entities. A player can break one by melee or projectile; ordinary damage
explodes it at power six, while an explosion removes it without another
explosion. A dragon cannot hurt one. Beam targets, the bottom plate and
invulnerability survive a restart.

Acceptance:

- Placement uses the clicked base, one air cell above and the vanilla entity
  exclusion box; failure consumes no item. Survival success consumes one.
- Official packet and placement/damage fixtures match the pinned jar.
- Changes use the existing section store and group frame, with no new owner
  process or transfer protocol. The fight's reactions are VMC-045a's next slice.
- Client handoff with the fight: place on obsidian, refuse an occupied base,
  shoot a spike crystal and strike a placed one; observe both explosions.

Reference: pinned Mojang 26.3 jar
`33680f5f2ac32864d6d7cf5e56a705fdb3e05f4c`, `EndCrystalItem.useOn`,
`EndCrystal.hurtServer`, `defineSynchedData`, `readAdditionalSaveData` and
`EndSpikeFeature.placeSpike`.

The generated entity fact table now includes the crystal's two synchronized
fields. Each crystal uses the existing object record, including imported
beam/bottom data. Placement checks the owning group's authoritative entity
boxes and creates its record in the hosted player's item-use frame. In the
End it keeps fire at its feet. Projectiles include it in their collision
targets; melee and projectile hits share its damage admission. Explosions
remove ordinary crystals without triggering another explosion.

Two shared ordering defects were exposed by a crystal hit. Hosted player
effects from an inline world call now wait until the current callback
settles, then run in order before the group cuts its frame; a callback may
defer at most 4,096. This preserves its current session and output buffer.
A projectile's terminal removal uses its current entry (a hit's explosion
may have pushed it) before running terminal effects, so it cannot hit itself
or survive on a stale revision. The existing pearl socket fixture also
closes the moved player's session and destination neighbours before deleting
its disposable dataset.
