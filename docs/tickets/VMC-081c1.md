# VMC-081c1 — Nearest-first generation

Status: Awaiting client
Milestone: M3, at the user's request
Parent: [VMC-081c](VMC-081c.md).

Capture 12 (081c on the VPS) was faster but, when slow, the user saw chunks
generate away from them while the chunk ahead or below stayed missing. The
group also held 282 sections and 14,473 chunks (four times the view) and ran
at about 10 TPS, which is 081c2 and the native core's.

## What the chunk beside the player waited for

The harness on one pinned core with two generation threads, flying at 30
blocks per second and turning a quarter every 25 seconds (`FLY_TURN`), stood
in a missing chunk for 21.9 of 120 seconds. While it waited, its own
decoration had run, five of its 5 × 5 lacked terrain, and terrain more than
two chunks away ran 17.9 s against 1.9 s beside it; the next terrain the
scheduler would pick was 6 to 77 chunks away.

- **Kind before distance.** `Frontier.next/3` started every final chunk, then
  every decoration any goal needed, and only then terrain, each in goal
  order: a far goal's ready decoration went before the near goal's terrain.
- **Request order.** Conflicting decorations ran in the order they were
  first requested (VMC-077x1). As the view's edge sweeps on, each newly
  requested decoration is later than its undecorated neighbours, so the
  decorations beside the player waited for a chain of earlier ones along the
  old edge, run one at a time from its far end. Turning added chains of
  decorations nobody needed any more, which kept their numbers.

## Changes

- Jobs start in goal order whatever their kind: for one goal its full chunk
  or chunk made alone, its decorations, then their terrain; other needed work
  after every goal's.
- Live, conflicting decorations run in the order they start. A running
  decoration holds its 3 × 3, so two within 2 still never run together; what
  is lost is only that a chunk's content was independent of job timing,
  which vanilla's is not either. The order log records decorations as they
  finish, which a reference replays. Request order stays as an option
  (`:generation_order`, `:requested`) for the oracle checks, which decorate
  in a fixed order and compare every block: with start order, 325 of 640
  final chunks differed where features cross chunk borders.
- Decoration numbers go nearest a player first within a request, and a
  decoration nobody needs gives its number up unless running.
- The pool runs one job more than there are threads, terrain at most one
  per thread, so no queued far terrain sits ahead of near work; spill
  encoding goes after queued work.

## Results

Same template, harness and container as 081c; one pinned core, two threads,
turning flight at 30 blocks per second, 120 seconds:

| Time in a missing chunk | 21.9 s | 17.4 s | 0 s |
| Chunks sent | 8,631 | 9,453 | 10,764 |
| Terrain jobs | 6,412 | 6,953 | 5,471 |
| Spills | 2,102 | 2,334 | 492 |
| TPS (mean / lowest second) | 19.7 / 14.0 | 19.8 / 15.9 | 19.9 / 17.0 |

Two pinned cores flying straight at 60 blocks per second (081c's run): the
client waited 21.4 s before and never after, flying 3,606 blocks against
2,325, with 6,608 chunks against 6,783 and 20 TPS.

The group size in capture 12 did not reproduce: in these runs it held about
100 sections throughout, loading and unloading as the player moved.
