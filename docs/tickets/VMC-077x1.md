# VMC-077x1 — Generation demand and order as one model

Status: Done
Milestone: foundations II (between M2 and M3)
Depends on: [VMC-077n](VMC-077n.md) (one generation scheduler per dimension),
[VMC-077u](VMC-077u.md) (generation follows the player), [VMC-077w](VMC-077w.md)
(deliveries with credit), [VMC-077x](VMC-077x.md) (generation does not stall at
the edge).

Before: priority enters the scheduler twice and is stored. Sections send their
wants as `chunk => squared distance to the ticket's center` (tickets carry a
center only for this), and each connection sends its player's chunk and heading
(`Scheduler.focus/5`); a wanted chunk's priority is the focus score when a
player is focused, else the center distance. The Frontier stores that priority
per wanted chunk and passes it down to every decoration and terrain chunk the
wanted chunk needs, so every chunk a player crosses changes priorities
everywhere. VMC-077x therefore added an incremental path (`reprioritize`,
`lower`, `lower_needs`, `@near`, `@urgent`) beside the whole rebuild
(`refresh/1`), paced by the scheduler, and a readiness fix (db03c751) for a
missing event that pacing exposed. Deliveries keep three maps (`requesters`,
`in_flight`, `outbox`), eviction sorts every cell per pass and needs a `stuck`
hysteresis, and `frontier.ex` holds every concern in 1,250 lines.

Vanilla derives load, status and priority from one ticket level per chunk;
Paper's player chunk loader orders generation radially from each player. Here
the decoration numbering adds a dependency chain vanilla does not have, so the
order has to reach it, but it can be derived instead of stored.

## Design

1. **Demand is membership.** A section wants a set of absent chunks, some of
   them urgent (calls that wait): `chunk => urgent?`. Tickets carry no center.
   Withdrawal is removal from the set. Calls (`full_chunks`, spawn search) want
   urgently.
2. **Priority is a pure function of the players.** A wanted chunk not sent yet
   (a goal) ranks urgent first, then by `Goals.score/2` from the nearest
   focused player, and with no player in key order (Z, then X). Goals live in
   one ordered set: a membership change inserts or deletes, a player crossing a
   chunk re-scores the set once, before the next dispatch. Nothing below the
   goals stores a priority.
3. **Dependencies are a graph, resolved at dispatch.** Each numbered decoration
   knows what it still waits for: the earlier numbered decorations within 2
   (all those numbered within 2 when it got its number) and the chunks of its
   3 × 3 without a cell. Its `need` counts the goals within 1 and the needed
   later decorations waiting for it: it is needed while a goal reaches it.
   `ready` holds the needed decorations that can start, and `terrain` counts,
   per chunk, the needed decorations lacking it. A terrain result, a
   decoration result, a dropped cell and a goal gained or lost update only
   what they touch; players' moves touch none of it. `free/2` is the one way a
   number goes (decorated, or blocked by a frozen chunk) and re-checks the
   decorations within 2 that waited for it. A dispatch starts final and alone
   goals in goal order, then walks the goals in order through their
   dependencies (their decorations, the earlier ones those wait for, the
   terrain those lack), memoized within the walk, until each kind's room is
   filled or all of it that can start was found, at most 1,000 steps; room
   left goes to the other needed work, ready decorations by number first. The
   numbering rule is unchanged, so decoration order and the vanilla live
   checks are identical.
4. **One delivery record per group process**: its monitor, its sections'
   epochs, the full chunks ready for it (each with the epoch it was made for)
   and its unacknowledged deliveries as `{key, chunk} => epoch` maps. What is
   on its way to a section is derived from it. A chunk queued under an older
   epoch keeps that epoch; a delivery with one unusable chunk fails only that
   chunk.
5. **Eviction from an LRU index** of the cells in memory: a pass after cells
   came into memory takes the least recently used first, looks at no more than
   64, and moves the cells work needs soon (held, a goal's, within 1 of a
   needed decoration) to the recent end, so a pass costs what it looks at; no
   hysteresis.
6. **`frontier.ex` split by concern**: goals and priority (`Frontier.Goals`),
   statuses and dependency resolution (`Frontier.Dependencies`), bounded
   memory (`Frontier.Memory`) and job closures (`Frontier.Runners`).

## Findings

Resolving dependencies at dispatch needs to know when to stop. Walking the
goals in order until the room is filled is cheap while work is near the front,
but a first version that knew only how many ready decorations exist walked
every goal (about 2,500 at view distance 32, each looking at its 3 × 3)
whenever nothing more could start: 642 µs of dispatch per job, 66 s of a
150 s flight at 116 blocks/s, the scheduler 68 % busy and a 22.9 s own-chunk
wait (a scheduler stack profile put `goals_in_order` at half its busy time).
Two counts make the stop exact without storing any priority: each numbered
decoration's `need` (goals and needed later decorations reaching it) and, per
chunk, the needed decorations lacking its terrain; they change only with
membership and results. With them the walk stops once it found all that can
start, and is skipped when nothing can.

A synthetic flight against the pure Frontier (instant jobs, a player crossing
a chunk every 20 dispatches, so the backlog fills the view) still showed the
cost of re-walking a front of goals blocked on their decoration chains at
every dispatch: 1.4 ms mean per `next/3`, growing with the backlog. Bounding
a walk to 1,000 steps and giving the room it leaves to the other needed work
(ready decorations by number, then needed terrain) made it flat at about
0.4 ms there, and about 100 µs per job in real flights. The scheduler
dispatches at once while two or more slots are free and otherwise once per
up to eight messages: dispatching only per eight messages left the pool
running four jobs or fewer with a backlog of over 500 chunks in 2 to 6 % of
samples (never so before), which made the longest own-chunk waits. Within a
goal the walk now visits the earlier decorations before taking a decoration's
own terrain: a chain advances only from its first decoration, and taking the
terrain of its last ones first (a depth-first walk from the goal) kept the
pool busy with work the own chunk could not yet use (a 5.5 s wait with the
pool full). One 7.4 s wait at 160 blocks/s was not generation: the group shed
sections (252 to 69) and nothing was wanted for 6 s, VMC-077x's open
follow-up, which group lifecycle work (VMC-077y) owns.

## Changes

- `Frontier.Goals`: wants as `chunk => urgent?`, the goal index and ordered
  goal set, `score/2`, re-scoring on focus. `Frontier.Dependencies`: statuses,
  numbering, each decoration's waits, lacking terrain and need, `ready`,
  `terrain`, `free/2`, `freeze/2`, and the walk (`resolve/3`).
  `Frontier.Memory`: the LRU index, eviction, spills. `Frontier.Runners`: job
  closures. Gone: `priority`, `needs`, `stale`, `refresh/1`, `reprioritize`,
  `lower`, `lower_needs`, `@near`, `@urgent` and the priority queues.
- The scheduler keeps one record per group (`groups`) instead of
  `requesters`, `in_flight` and `outbox`; it dispatches without rebuilds or
  pacing (at once while two or more pool slots are free, else once per up to
  eight messages), and runs an eviction pass after cells came into memory.
- `World.Tickets`, `Region.ticket/4` and a view's ticket batches carry no
  center; `RegionGeneration.wanted/2` is membership with urgency, and an
  unusable chunk in a delivery fails alone.
- The bench's `waits` sampler reads the new state.
